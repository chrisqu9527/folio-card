## 提示词

```
<inputs>
First ask me: short or long form?
- Short: 1080x1920 vertical, 30 fps, 45-70 s, one story, about 130-190 words.
- Long form: 1920x1080 horizontal, 30 fps, 8-15 min, chaptered, about 150 words per minute.
Then ask me for my topic, or pitch 3.
- A good one is a real, current money or business event that looks impossible at first: a giant loses billions in a morning, a market crashes on good news, a company pays to lose. The reveal is a chain of plain causes the viewer can follow.
- Give each pitch with its first line, the big number, the hidden cause, and a title phrased as a question ("Why the ___ Just ___").
Ask which voice I want:
- Free, the default: Kokoro, run locally on the CPU.
- Optional upgrade: ElevenLabs with a stock library voice. Tell me the credit cost before you generate anything, and make one take.
Tell me everything is free: Python, Node with Playwright and a headless browser, FFmpeg, faster-whisper, Pixabay music and Mixkit sound effects. No image, video or 3D model generator is used anywhere. Everything is drawn in code on an HTML canvas.
Then write the script and the beat list with seconds, and show me both before you build anything.
</inputs>

<direction>
A narrated "why did this just happen" money explainer in a flat 2D vector cartoon style: simple cartoon people and talking objects acting inside lit, dressed flat sets, one calm storyteller voice, numbers and labels drawn inside the world, black typed-text cards for the big questions, and a camera that slowly pushes on every hold.

Sets (2D, drawn in code on an HTML canvas):
- Build every place from simple flat shapes: rounded rectangles, circles, ellipses, bezier curves. No downloaded art, no AI images.
- Every shape has a medium dark outline (warm near-black, about 4-5 px at 1080 p) with round joins.
- Turn every idea into a physical object: a stock index becomes a skyline of bars, a rally becomes a balloon, a bubble is a real glass bubble with the company inside, a loss is a giant chip crushing its owner, a budget is a shopping list.
- Dress every place: windows, signs, pigeons, clocks, plants, papers, crowds. Never an empty set.
- Put the facts inside the world: price counters, signs on buildings, labels on boxes, clipboards, calendars, clocks with times.
Palette and light:
- Each place gets one mood: night green glow for tech, clean daylight blue for the street, off-white grid paper for charts, deep blue-grey spotlight for the question shots, red trading floors for panic.
- Light comes from inside the scene: soft radial light pools behind the subject, a spotlight cone, a glowing object. Add a vignette on every shot.
- Numbers: red with a soft red glow for losses, green for gains, white with a dark outline for names.
- Finish with a soft glow (screen-blend a blurred highlight pass at about 0.4 opacity).
People and objects:
- The look, kept simple:
  - a round skin-tone head with a soft shade on one side, outlined
  - small dark oval eyes with a white catch-light, simple brows, a tiny nose curve, a small mouth that changes shape
  - a bean-shaped body in one flat colour (suit, hoodie, uniform, lab coat), thin stick arms and legs, small dark shoe ovals, a soft floor shadow
  - one marker each so the viewer knows who it is: grey hair and glasses, a cap and badge, a white coat
- Companies can be talking objects (a fruit, a cloud, a box) with the same eyes and outline, never a real logo.
- Real public figures appear only as generic cartoon types (an exec in a black jacket), never a likeness.
- The face set: neutral, grin, smug, worried, shock (white ring eyes, open mouth), sweat drop.
- Pose-to-pose acting: arms up holding something, hands on hips, pointing, holding a clipboard. Blinks and small breathing keep holds alive.
Camera and editing:
- Hold each picture 2-4 s with a slow push-in or slow drift, then a hard cut on a word to the next picture. This style cuts often (about every 2-3 s); it never sits still.
- One small shake on each impact (a crash, a stamp).
- Black cards for the big questions: the line types on word by word in a white handwritten font (Caveat), centred, timed to the voice.
- Long form: open with a 30 s cold open (the shocking fact, the "nothing was wrong" beat, the numbers, the big question), then chapter cards on black ("The Top", "Crack One").
Text:
- In-world labels use a bold hand-drawn font (Kalam Bold) in caps, white or coloured fill with a thick dark outline.
- Counters animate: they tick up on the word that says the number.
- No burned-in subtitles; the words in the world and the black cards do that job.
Banned:
- AI images or video, downloaded art, real logos, real likenesses
- a camera that sits still for more than a second
- half-cut faces, legs-only frames, empty backgrounds
- a time-stretched or whispered voice
- jargon left unexplained
</direction>

<structure>
Short (about 60 s):
0-4 s, HOOK: the shocking number, shown happening ("lost $400 billion in a single morning").
4-12 s, NOTHING WAS WRONG: three quick "No ___" beats, one picture each.
12-25 s, THE DAMAGE: two more numbers, each as an object that breaks (a skyline crumbles, a balloon deflates against a stopwatch).
25-30 s, THE QUESTION: a black card that types the question.
30-50 s, THE CAUSE: two or three plain causes, one prop each, joined with "and" and "so".
50-60 s, THE TWIST: who actually won, shown, not explained. The last line calls back to the hook.
Long form (8-15 min):
-0:30 cold open as above, then "The short answer is..." in one line.
Then 4-6 chapters, each opened by a black chapter card: the setup with one exact number, then each cause as its own small story with one visual metaphor, then the day it broke minute by minute (clocks in the corner), then who won and what happens next.
Every 40-60 s, change the location and the colour mood so the eye stays fresh.
</structure>

<build>
1. Tools (all free): Python 3, Node with Playwright, FFmpeg, faster-whisper, numpy, plus the fonts Kalam and Caveat Install what's missing and tell me what you installed.
2. Script:
   - Tell it the way you'd explain it to a smart friend: plain words, exact numbers, one funny comparison per minute.
   - Join related sentences with commas, because a voice resets after every full stop and leaves a gap.
   - Short punchy fragments are fine for the "No bank failed. No law passed." beat.
   - Show me the word count and the seconds per line.
3. Voice:
   - Free route: Kokoro on the CPU, one line per call, joined with short gaps.
   - Paid route: ElevenLabs, one take, cost stated first.
   - Only trim silence, never inside a word. Then run faster-whisper on the finished track to get word times.
4. Characters and objects: draw the cast in code, show me a sheet, then build the scenes.
5. Shot table: one file that lists every shot with its start and end anchored to a word time, never a typed time. Every animation (counters, crumbles, typing, entrances) is also keyed to a word time.
6. Scene file: one draw function per shot, plus a camera function (zoom, focus point, small roll, shake) and helpers for outlines, labels, light pools and the vignette.
7. Exporter: a headless browser page draws every frame on a 1920x1080 (or 1080x1920) canvas and writes JPG frames, skipping frames that already exist so it can resume.
8. Contact sheet before the full render: a still from the middle of every shot, labelled with time and the line being spoken. I approve or ask for changes.
9. Sound:
   - Music: one low, tense-but-curious bed under a free licence, about 20 dB under the voice.
   - Effects: real Mixkit sounds on the actions (cash-register dings on counters, crumbles on crashes, a typewriter tick on black cards, whooshes on cuts).
   - Duck music and effects under speech. Normalise to -14 LUFS.
10. Finish with FFmpeg: the soft glow, the mix, H.264.
11. Give me a one-click .bat (or .sh) that runs the export and the finish. It must refuse to encode if any frame is missing, and never overwrite an older video.
12. Upload text:
   - Title: "Why [the thing] Just [happened]".
   - Thumbnail: the hook frame (the giant object crushing the worried character) with the big red number, no other text.
</build>

<gotchas>
Script:
- Starting with background (dates, names, context) loses people. Line 1 is the shocking number happening.
- Long form drifts if a chapter has no picture idea. Every paragraph needs a prop before it's written.
Look:
- Empty sets read as cheap. Dress every place.
- Objects that should be held (a chip, a sign) must touch the hands, or they float.
- Text on signs must fit its board; measure it and shrink it if needed.
- Labels over a prop get hidden; check every label is readable.
Camera:
-  is narrow: re-frame every shot for vertical, don't just crop the horizontal one.
- A pan that passes over half a building between beats is fine, but every hold must land on a full face or a full object.
Render:
- A render that stops early must not be encoded into a short video. Check the frame count first.
Sound:
- Music only a few dB under the voice makes the narrator hard to hear. Keep it low and measure.
</gotchas>

<start>
Ask me short or long form first. Then ask for my topic or pitch 3. Then show me the script (word count and seconds per line) and the beat list before any voice is generated or any scene is drawn.
</start>
```
